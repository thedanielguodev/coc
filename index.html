Research question

How accurately can AI estimate and interpret the relationship between wildfire-related destruction and homelessness across California CoCs, compared with the relationship observed in real HUD/CAL FIRE data? See the two panels below: fire → homelessness correlation (the ground truth) and AI vs. ground truth (whether an LLM shown only fire history infers the same weak relationship, or a stronger one).

Fire → homelessness correlation

—
Each point is one CoC in one year: acres burned within that CoC's boundary (x) vs. its homeless count (y). Points at x = 0 are CoC-years with no major (≥1,000-acre) fire on record. Line is the least-squares fit. Default timing is fire-year → next year's count (t → t+1), on the theory that displacement shows up in the following PIT count rather than the same one; toggle to "Same year" to compare. Large urban CoCs dominate homeless totals but rarely burn heavily, while large rural CoCs burn heavily but have small populations — this population/geography mismatch pulls the raw correlation toward zero independent of any real relationship, so treat r as a starting point, not a verdict.

AI vs. ground truth

—
Each point is one CoC. X is the real per-CoC Pearson r (same-year lag) between fire acreage and homeless count, computed directly from the joined data. Y is an LLM's estimate of that same figure, made from that CoC's wildfire history alone — it was never shown the actual homeless counts. Points above the dashed y = x line are CoCs where the model overstated fire's relationship to homelessness versus the real data; points below understated it. Generated by ai_estimate_influence.py (needs an ANTHROPIC_API_KEY) or a blind Claude Code sub-agent given only fire histories, then compare_ai_vs_ground_truth.py; this panel is blank until those have been run. See CLAUDE.md for how these estimates were produced.
